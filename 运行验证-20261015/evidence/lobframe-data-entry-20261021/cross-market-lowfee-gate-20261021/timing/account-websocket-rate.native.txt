L17: # Account Types
L18: 
L19: Lighter API users can operate under a Standard, Plus or Premium/Advanced account.
L20: 
L21: > 📘
L22: >
L23: > Binary options fees use separate volume and LIT staking tiers. Fetch them through cite4†feeTiers with `filter=bo`; read the account's `bo_fee_rate` from cite5†accountLimits . The trading-fee tables below do not determine binary options fees. See cite6†Binary options: Trading and fees .
L24: ####
L25: 
L26: Premium/Advanced Account (Opt-in) -- Suitable for HFT, the lowest latency on Lighter. Part of cite7†volume quota program .
L27: 
L28: Latency for maker & cancel orders is 0ms. Fee credits also count as staked LIT for the parameters described in the table.

L48: ####
L49: 
L50: Standard Account (Default) -- Suitable for retail and latency-insensitive traders.
L51: 
L52: Maker Fee  | Taker Fee  | Taker Latency  | Cancel/Modify Latency  | Maker Latency  | Sub-accounts
L53: --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---
L54: 0%  | 0%  | 300 ms  | 300 ms  | 0ms  | 4
L55: 
L56: For standard accounts only, TWAP orders charge a 1bps fee.
L57: ####
L58: 
L59: Account Switch
L60: 
L61: You can change your Account Type (tied to your L1 address) using the cite8†`/changeAccountTier` endpoint.
L62: 
L63: You may call that endpoint if at least 24 hours have passed since the last call. To upgrade account type, there is no cooldown.

L159: 
L160: How fees are collected:
L161: 
L162: In isolated margin, fees are taken from the isolated position itself, but if needed, we automatically transfer from cross margin to keep the position healthy. In cross margin, fees are always deducted directly from the available cross balance.
L163: 
L164: Sub-accounts share the same tier as the main L1 address on the account.

L18: # WebSocket
L19: 
L20: This page will help you get started with zkLighter WebSocket server.
L21: #
L22: 
L23: Connection
L24: 
L25: URL: `wss://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/stream`; `wss://testnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/stream`
L26: 
L27: You can directly connect to the WebSocket server using wscat:
L28: 
L29: [Button: Shell]
L30: 
L31:     `wscat -c 'wss://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/stream'`
L32: 
L33: If you're connecting from a restricted region, you can still access read-only data:
L34: 
L35: [Button: Shell]
L36: 
L37:     `wscat -c 'wss://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/stream?readonly=true'`
L38: #
L39: 
L40: Keepalive Requirements
L41: 
L42: Clients are responsible for keeping the connection alive by sending at least one frame every 2 minutes. This can be either:
L43: 
L44:   * A WebSocket ping frame
L45:   * Any application-level message
L46: 
L47: Clients sending `{"type":"ping"}` will receive back `{"type":"pong"}`.
L48: 
L49: If the server receives no frames from a client within the 2-minute window, it will close the connection.
L50: 
L51: Support for `permessage-deflate` compression is enabled.
L52: Clients that fall behind on reading messages may be disconnected more aggressively than before.

L17: # Rate Limits
L18: 
L19: We enforce rate limits on both REST API and WebSocket usage. These limits apply to both IP address and L1 address. It's important to note that for non-standard account types, sendTx and sendTxBatch fall into a separate rate limit bucket cite4†described below , and are cite5†linked to account type and staked tokens .
L20: 
L21: * * *
L22: ##
L23: 
L24: REST API Endpoint Limits
L25: 
L26: The following limits apply to the `https://mainnet.zklighter.elliot.ai/api/v1/` base URL, excluding `sendTx` and `sendTxBatch` where different limits, listed cite4†below , apply. Different limits, also listed cite6†below , apply to `https://explorer.elliot.ai/api/`. To bypass IP-based rate limits, clients can authenticate each request so that only L1-based rate limits apply.
L27: Builder accounts  | Plus accounts  | Premium/Advanced accounts  | Standard accounts
L28: --- | --- | --- | ---
L29: 240,000 weighted requests per rolling minute  | 24,000 weighted requests per rolling minute  | 24,000 weighted requests per rolling minute  | 60 requests per rolling minute
L30: ###
L31: 
L32: Weights:
L33: 
L34: Endpoint  | Weight
L35: --- | ---
L36: `sendTx, sendTxBatch, nextNonce`  | 6
L37: `publicPools, txFromL1TxHash`  | 50
L38: `accountInactiveOrders, accountActiveOrders, accountOrders, deposit/latest`  | 100
L39: `exchangeMetrics`  | 120
L40: `apikeys`  | 150
L41: `transferFeeInfo`  | 500
L42: `trades`  | 200
L43: `recentTrades`  | 600
L44: `changeAccountTier, tokens/*, setAccountMetadata, notification/ack, createIntentAddress, fastwithdraw, referral/*`  | 3000
L45: `tokens/create`  | 23000
L46: Other endpoints  | 300
L47: While standard accounts rate limits are not weighted, whenever `{premium_weighted_requests}/{endpoint_weight} < {standard_requests}`, the former limit is going to be applied. For example, both Standard and Premium/Advanced accounts will be able to make a maximum of 8 requests per rolling minute to the `changeAccountTier` endpoint.
L48: You can apply for a Builder Account through our Discord support channel. It’s free of charge, but we reserve the right to review applications and verify the intended use. Builder Accounts include higher limits for querying our read-only REST API endpoints, excluding `sendTx` and `sendTxBatch`; otherwise, standard account limits apply. Builders on this tier should authenticate every request, even when not explicitly required in our documentation.
L49: 
L50: * * *
L51: ##
L52: 
L53: WebSocket Limits
L54: 
L55: To prevent resource exhaustion, we enforce the following usage limits per IP:
L56:   * Connections: 255
L57:   * Subscriptions per connection: 500
L58:   * Subscriptions per IP: 5000
L59:   * Unique Accounts per connection: 500
L60:   * Unique Accounts per IP: 5000
L61:   * Max Connections Per Minute: 255 (not to be confused with channel subscriptions)
L62:   * Max Messages Sent By Client Per Minute: 200 (sendTx and sendBatchTx are not counted here, and follow the same limits as REST requests)
L63:   * Max Inflight Messages: 50 (sendTx and sendBatchTx are not counted here)
L64: Deployments with no downtime might drop your connection, so it's recommended to have proper reconnection logic, in addition to ping/pong logic.

L161: ##
L162: 
L163: Rate Limit Exceeding Behavior
L164: 
L165: If you exceed any rate limit:
L166:   * You will receive `HTTP 429`, or `HTTP 405`
L167:   * For WebSocket connections, excessive messages may result in disconnection
L168:   * When you're rate-limited on REST, WebSocket connections also get rate-limited, and vice versa
L169:   * For Premium/Advanced and Plus accounts, mainnet endpoints fall into a separate bucket from `sendTx` and `sendTxBatch`, meaning that getting rate-limited in one of the two buckets will not affect activity on the other. Put simply, sending too many transactions in a minute will not affect your ability to fetch data from the exchange, and vice versa.
L170: To avoid this, please ensure your clients are implementing proper backoff and retry strategies.
L171: ##
L172: 
L173: Cooldown
L174: 
L175: Depending on whether you have been rate-limited by our firewall, or by the api servers, the cooldown period varies:
L176: 
L177:   * Firewall: 60 seconds, static
L178:   * Api servers: `weightOfEndpoint/(totalWeight/60)`
L179: 
L180: As an example, making a request to the `account` endpoint (which carries a `weight` of 300) after having exhausted your weighted requests in a given minute window, results in a 750ms cooldown period.
