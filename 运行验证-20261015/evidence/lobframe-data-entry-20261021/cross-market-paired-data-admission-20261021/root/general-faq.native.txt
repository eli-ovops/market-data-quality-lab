L6: ## About Tardis.dev
L7: ### What is Tardis.dev and what is your unique value proposition?
L8: 
L9: Tardis.dev provides tick-level historical and real-time cryptocurrency market data from 50+ exchanges and offers:
L10: * access to [high frequency historical data](/faq/data.md#what-does-high-frequency-historical-data-mean), including tick-level order book updates and trades for [derivatives and top spot cryptocurrency exchanges](#which-exchanges-instruments-and-currency-pairs-are-supported)
L11: * [non-standard data types](/faq/data.md#what-data-types-do-you-support): historical funding, open interest, indexes, liquidations and more
L12: * access via [API](/api/quickstart.md) and [downloadable CSV files](/downloadable-csv-files/overview.md)
L13: * data [sourced from real-time WebSocket market data feeds](/faq/data.md#why-data-source-matters-websocket-feeds-vs-rest-endpoints), with documented [collection details](/historical-data-details.md#market-data-collection-overview)
L14: * [exchange-native](/faq/data.md#what-is-a-difference-between-exchange-native-and-normalized-data-format) and [normalized data format](/faq/data.md#what-is-a-difference-between-exchange-native-and-normalized-data-format) support
L15: * [time-machine market replay](#how-does-time-machine-market-replay-work) for reconstructing order book state at historical points in time
L16: * [always available market data backup](#what-is-your-infrastructure-setup)
L17: * [subscription types](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#what-are-the-differences-between-subscription-types) for solo traders, small prop shops, businesses and academic researchers
L18: * [consolidated real-time market data streaming](#do-you-support-consolidated-real-time-market-data-streaming) support via Tardis.dev open source libraries that connect directly to exchanges' public WebSocket APIs (no API key required)
L19: **Data use cases**
L20: 
L21: * market microstructure and order book dynamics research
L22: * trading execution optimization
L23: * tick-level granularity market simulation
L24: * liquidity and lead-lag analysis
L25: * backtesting and optimization of trading strategies
L26: * full historical order book reconstruction at any given point in time
L27: * alpha generation
L28: * designing quantitative models
L29: * academic research
L30: * data visualizations
L31: ### Why would I use your services if I can collect data by myself?
L32: 
L33: You can collect exchange market data directly, but doing it reliably is time-consuming and resource-intensive. It requires collection infrastructure, storage, distribution, monitoring, exchange API maintenance, new exchange integrations, and ongoing handling of connection drops, rate limits, latency spikes, and upstream API changes. Supported exchanges publish around 1 TB of new data per day.
L34: We maintain that infrastructure so you can focus on trading and research instead of building an in-house data collection and distribution system. See our [data types](/faq/data.md#what-data-types-do-you-support), [data sourcing approach](/faq/data.md#why-data-source-matters-websocket-feeds-vs-rest-endpoints), and [subscription types](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#what-are-the-differences-between-subscription-types) for details.

L198: ## Using the data
L199: ### What can I use the data for?
L200: 
L201: For your own internal business, research, educational or personal use, such as backtesting, strategy development, trading and risk management, including work for your clients and task-specific quantitative models you build for that work. You can't redistribute or resell the data, or use it to provide a product or service that gives others access to it, such as a data feed, an API or a backtesting platform.
L202: You can share charts, statistics, research reports and papers made from the data, and aggregated results such as OHLCV candles of 10 minutes or longer, when the raw data can't be reconstructed from them; this doesn't apply to Coinbase data. You can store and process the data with cloud and IT providers that keep it confidential and don't use it for their own purposes.
L203: ### Can I keep using the data after my subscription ends?
L204: 
L205: Yes. Data you downloaded while you had access, including trial and free data, stays licensed to you after your subscription ends, under the same licence restrictions.

L207: ## Getting the data
L208: ### How can I download the data?
L209: You can access historical market data via [API](/api/quickstart.md) which provides raw data in [exchange native format](/faq/data.md#what-is-a-difference-between-exchange-native-and-normalized-data-format) or download [CSV datasets](/downloadable-csv-files/overview.md) with [trades](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#trades), [incremental order book L2 updates](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#incremental_book_l2), order book snapshots ([top 25](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#book_snapshot_25) and [top 5](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#book_snapshot_5)), [options chains](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#options_chain), [quotes](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#quotes), [book tickers](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#book_ticker), [derivative tickers](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#derivative_ticker) (open interest, funding, mark price, index price) and [liquidations](/downloadable-csv-files/data-types.md#liquidations).
L210: [Our client libs](/api/quickstart.md) provide data in [normalized format](/faq/data.md#what-is-a-difference-between-exchange-native-and-normalized-data-format) as well, which can be more flexible than CSV datasets for some use cases but also slower to download due to on-demand, client-side data normalization overhead in comparison to ready to download CSV files.
L211: {% hint style="info" %}
L212: **Replay API** supports custom time windows with minute precision (e.g., `2024-01-15T09:30:00.000Z` to `2024-01-15T16:00:00.000Z`). **CSV datasets** are available as daily files.
L213: {% endhint %}
L214: ### What can affect CSV dataset download speed?
L215: 
L216: CSV dataset download speed depends mainly on the network path between your location and our primary cloud storage in the US East region. Longer cross-continent routes, local network conditions and temporary public Internet congestion can affect throughput.
L217: Very rarely, a cloud storage service may be temporarily slower or unavailable. We recommend using the latest [official Python or Node.js clients](/downloadable-csv-files/overview.md#download-via-client-libraries). They download multiple files concurrently and automatically retry transient network or storage errors.
L218: ### What programming languages are supported?
L219: 
L220: Any programming language that can communicate using HTTPS can communicate with our [HTTP API](/api/http-api-reference.md).
L221: 
L222: We provide official [Python](/python-client/quickstart.md) and [Node.js](/node-client/quickstart.md) clients for accessing tick-level historical market data.
L223: Finally, our open source, locally runnable [tardis-machine](/tardis-machine/quickstart.md) server with built-in local data caching provides market data normalization, custom order book snapshot capabilities, and real-time market data streaming support that connects directly to exchanges' WebSocket APIs.
L224: It provides both streaming HTTP and WebSocket endpoints returning market data for whole time periods (in contrast to Tardis.dev [HTTP API](/api/http-api-reference.md), where a single call returns 1 to 10 minutes of data) and is available via npm and as a Docker image.
L225: ### What API protocols can be used to access market data?
L226: 
L227: Historical market data provided by [HTTP API](/api/http-api-reference.md) can be accessed via HTTPS.
L228: 
L229: Locally runnable [tardis-machine](/tardis-machine/quickstart.md) server provides both HTTP and WebSocket based APIs for accessing both historical and real-time market data.
L230: ### How does time-machine market replay work?
L231: Exchange WebSocket APIs are built for real-time feeds, not historical replay. Locally runnable [Tardis Machine](/tardis-machine/replaying-historical-data.md#websocket-ws-replay-exchange-exchange-and-from-fromdate-and-to-todate) bridges that gap by replaying historical exchange-native data through a WebSocket API with the same subscribe-style flow as exchange real-time APIs. In many cases, an existing exchange WebSocket client can replay a historical date range by changing the endpoint URL.
L232: ### Do you support consolidated real-time market data streaming?
L233: 
L234: We do not provide a hosted real-time market data API. Exchanges already provide public real-time WebSocket feeds directly, and a hosted Tardis.dev real-time API would add latency and another dependency.
L235: 
L236: Use the [open source clients and locally runnable Tardis Machine](/api/quickstart.md) for consolidated real-time streaming. They are self-hosted, free to use, and connect directly to exchange WebSocket APIs without a Tardis.dev API key.
L237: ### Are there any rate-limits for the API?
L238: 
L239: Yes. See [API Rate Limits](/api/rate-limits.md) for account-level API request and data transfer limits.

L241: ## API keys
L242: ### How do I obtain my API key?
L243: 
L244: Ask an [AI agent](/agents-and-ai.md), or choose **Create Data API key** in your [account](https://tardis.dev/account#api-keys). You need a [subscription](https://tardis.dev/#order) or a [free trial](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#how-do-free-trials-work) first. Copy the key when it's shown, because you won't see it again.
L245: ### How can I rotate or reset my API key?
L246: 
L247: Ask an [AI agent](/agents-and-ai.md), or choose **Rotate** next to the key in your [account](https://tardis.dev/account#api-keys). You get a new key at once, and the previous one keeps working for 1 hour; an account can rotate keys 3 times in 24 hours. If a key was compromised, choose **Revoke** to stop it immediately.
L248: ### Why doesn't my API key work?
L249: 
L250: The error message says why:
L251: * "This account does not have access to…": the exchange or date you requested is outside your subscription's [data plan](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#how-does-subscription-based-access-work) or [historical data range](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#how-much-historical-data-does-my-subscription-include), or outside a [free trial's](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#how-do-free-trials-work) days 1–10 of the previous month. Solo and Academic subscriptions include CSV files only.
L252: * "Your account has no data access yet…", "Your free trial has expired…" or "Your subscription access has ended…": the account has no active trial or subscription. A subscription paid by bank transfer starts when the payment arrives; after a failed renewal, see [What happens if a renewal payment fails?](/faq/billing-and-subscriptions.md#what-happens-if-a-renewal-payment-fails)
L253: * "This Data API key is already active from another IP address": except with Business subscriptions, a key works from [one IP address at a time](/api/rate-limits.md#api-key-usage). Another IP address can use it 15 minutes after the last request from the first one.
L254: * "This account has reached its data transfer limit": see your usage and reset date in your account and the [data transfer limits](/api/rate-limits.md#data-transfer-limits).
L255: * "This account is sending Data Feed requests too quickly": retry after the seconds in the `retry-after` header, and see the [request limits](/api/rate-limits.md#request-limits).
L256: * "Unauthorized": the key is missing, mistyped, revoked, or was rotated more than an hour ago. Send it as `Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_KEY`.
